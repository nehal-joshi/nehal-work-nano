---
title: "Life as a CV"
description: "The future belongs to those who build and document their learning journeys. Starting early is the key."
pubDate: 2024-12-05
---
<p>As learning becomes more accessible on the internet almost everyone now will have some degree or a certificate from a top-tier institute. What happens when education is democratized, and there are a million others with the same degree or diploma as yours?<br><br>Thanks to ChatGPT, now every resume looks finely crafted and optimised for a high ATS score. Adding more difficulties for recruiters to filter through and find good candidates. So, how does one stand out?<br><br>I think the answer to both the questions is - build project-based portfolios. Show your proof of skills! Convert knowledge and skills into tangible projects.<br><br>Building your portfolio is something, like design thinking, that the world can learn from the field of design. It is the best way to apply and demonstrate your skills in a real-world context. It’s a great way of documenting your learning journey for yourself and the world to see.<br><br>A portfolio does not necessarily mean only your design or art works. A portfolio can be a set of articles written by you, analysis of products or market trends, documented experiments done in a chemistry lab, or even a video playlist.<br><br>It’s not that only when you are seeking a job you should build a portfolio. This portfolio building can start as early as primary school. An idea discussed in Blume’s edtech report in 2022 is to build a holistic ‘Life as a CV’. An approach that can lead to building a habit of creation, problem solving, effective communication, and a design thinking mindset, setting up stage for entrepreneurship / innovation.<br><br>Platforms like Peerlist are doing a great job of encouraging students and professionals to build side projects regularly. We need something similar for K12 education where students not only get a platform for building their projects portfolio but also get a community, mentorship, and learning opportunities beyond academics. IMO this will be the next big trend in K12.<br><br>What are your thoughts on this?</p>
